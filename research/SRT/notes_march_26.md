### The Math: Reverse Engineering £31m PBT
To hit a **£31m Profit Before Tax (PBT)** on Cavendish's forecasted **£123m FY27 Revenue**, here is what SRT's P&L must look like:

*   **PBT Target:** £31m
*   *Add back Interest/Finance Costs:* ~£2m (They have £13.6m in 10% loan notes and other debt).
*   **Operating Profit Required:** ~£33m 
*   *Add back Operating Expenses (Admin Costs):* ~£22m (H1 FY26 was £10.2m with 161 staff; scaling to 200 staff + inflation safely puts FY27 OpEx at ~£22m-25m).
*   **Gross Profit Required:** ~£55m to £58m

**The Catch: The Implied Gross Margin**
Generating £55m of Gross Profit on £123m of revenue requires a **Blended Gross Margin of ~45%**. 

### Is a 45% Gross Margin Absurd?
Historically, **yes.** Currently, **maybe not.** 
*   **FY24 Margin:** 28%
*   **FY25 Margin:** 31%
*   **H1 FY26 Margin:** 27% (management noted this dip was due to heavy, low-margin infrastructure mix like concrete and towers).

However, management's own guidance states that their Nav Safety division (transceivers) runs at **42-45% gross margin**, and their Systems division contracts range from **15% to 40%+.** 

### Conclusion: How Cavendish gets there
For Cavendish to be **right**, they are modeling a massive structural shift in the revenue mix for FY27. They assume that the low-margin, heavy-lifting physical installations (towers, boats, concrete) for the Middle East contracts will be completed in FY26. 

By FY27, they expect the revenue mix to shift heavily toward:
1. High-margin software licenses (GeoVS).
2. AI data analytics/intelligence tokens.
3. System support and maintenance. 

If SRT successfully transitions these large sovereign clients into the software/data lifecycle phase, a 45% margin is achievable—meaning the £31m PBT happens. 

If Cavendish is **wrong**, it's because physical project implementation gets delayed, keeping margins dragged down in the 25-30% range. At a 30% margin on £123m revenue, Gross Profit is only £37m. After £22m in OpEx and £2m in interest, PBT would only be **~£13m**—not £31m.

Based on the documents provided in the SRT folder, here is the breakdown of how recurring revenue is structured for SRT Marine Systems:

There is no exact percentage or exact monetary figure given for recurring revenue. However, the documents provide a clear picture of how much of their revenue profile is recurring versus one-off:

Currently, the vast majority of revenue is "Lumpy" and Milestone-Based (Not strictly recurring)

The Systems Division accounts for ~90% of revenue, and its recent explosive growth (+426% in FY25, +95% in H1 FY26) is driven by the simultaneous delivery of massive infrastructure contracts (towers, radars, civil works, core software installations).
This core revenue is recognized on a milestone basis (tied to physical deliverables), which management explicitly acknowledges makes the revenue profile "lumpy" from half to half, rather than a smooth recurring line.
The Transition to a "Recurring Revenue Platform" Despite the milestone-heavy nature of the initial contract installations, the documents highlight that SRT is transitioning into what the analyst report calls a "blue-chip-quality recurring revenue platform." The recurring streams layered into these contracts include:

10-20 Year Annuity Streams: Every sovereign partnership signed creates a long-term tail of locked-in maintenance, support, and incremental system expansion contracts.
Hardware-as-a-Service (USVs/UAVs): SRT provides unmanned autonomous vessels (like the Kuwait USV program) on a leased/service basis, which management describes as a "repeatable, long term recurring revenue model."
Enhanced Data Services: Things like satellite surveillance and AI-processed intelligence tokens act as an ongoing, high-margin subscription layered on top of the installed platform.
Navigation Safety Division (~10% of revenue) This division relies on sales of hardware (like the new NEXUS multi-comms transceiver) through a network of 5,000 global dealers. While this isn't contracted "recurring" revenue (like a SaaS product), it functions as a highly consistent and predictable "repeat" revenue stream with a large addressable market.

Summary: While the bulk of the headline revenue numbers hitting the P&L right now are large, one-off milestone payments for system installations, each of those installations locks the customer into a decades-long ecosystem of high-margin recurring services and maintenance.
