### The 5 Big Reveals

**1. Explicit EUV unit guidance — first time ever**
- FY2026: "at least **60 Low-NA EUV**"
- FY2027: "at least **80 Low-NA EUV**" (capacity being raised now)
- Supply chain prepared for up to **90 EUV + 600 DUV**
- Roger: "80 tools give you **double the capacity** we shipped in 2025 on 44 tools"

**2. Non-EUV upgraded from flat to growth**
- Immersion supply chain bottleneck fixed → output now "close to FY25 levels"
- This was the primary driver of the guidance raise
- Growth going to non-China customers; China stays at ~20%

**3. FY2027 ASP will improve**
- FY2026 mix: ~80% NXE:3800E, ~20% NXE:3600D
- FY2027 mix: "hardly any D's, lion's share E's, a number of F's"
- Roger confirmed: "clearly the ASP next year will improve over this year"
- NXE:3800F spec raised from 250 to **260 WpH**, ships 2027, full volume 2028

**4. Memory sold out through 2026 and beyond**
- Christophe: "many customers confirmed they are **sold out for the remainder of the year** and expect supply limitation to persist beyond 2026"
- Logic customers also adding capacity across multiple nodes and expect supply limitation beyond 2026
- Both supported by long-term agreements with their own customers

**5. CMD 2030 targets likely too conservative**
- Analyst asked if tracking toward upper end of €44-60B for 2030
- Management deferred to 2027 CMD rather than deny it
- DRAM capacity additions running above the 160K WSPM/year assumption
- Christophe: "things have changed a bit with AI... we have to go back and look at those numbers again"

**Financial Performance and Margins**
In the first quarter of fiscal year 2026 (Q1 FY26), ASML achieved a strong gross margin of 53%. This uptick in profitability was primarily driven by an increase in component sales originating from the company's installed base management segment. 

**Margin Outlook and Strategic Investments**
Looking ahead to the remainder of the year, ASML anticipates its gross margin will stabilize between 51% and 53%. While the company expects a financial boost from an increase in its immersion lithography business, these gains will be offset by near-term strategic investments. Specifically, ASML is actively expanding its workforce and absorbing the operational costs associated with ramping up overall production capacity.

**Market Dynamics and AI Demand**
Looking at the broader market landscape, the overall demand for artificial intelligence infrastructure continues to outstrip available supply. This sustained imbalance highlights the critical need for advanced semiconductor manufacturing capacity to support the rapidly expanding AI ecosystem.

**Production Capacity and EUV Ramping**
To keep pace with this surging customer demand, ASML is significantly accelerating its production capacity. A major focal point of their strategy is the aggressive ramp-up of Low NA EUV (Extreme Ultraviolet) systems. The company confirmed it is executing an output plan to deliver at least 60 Low NA EUV units in 2026. Furthermore, ASML plans to scale this capacity to at least 80 systems by 2027 to properly support the industry's rapidly expanding infrastructure needs.

**Capacity Management and Strategic Risks**
Despite the aggressive output targets for 2026 and 2027, there is a recognized risk that ASML may actually undership against total market demand during this period. This potential shortfall stems from a calculated internal resistance to overextending their manufacturing footprint. Management is taking a cautious approach to scaling, deliberately pacing their ramp-up to prevent any long-term overcapacity issues once the current surge normalizes.

**Non-EUV Revenue Outlook**
Regarding the company's non-EUV operations, earlier projections indicated that revenue would remain flat throughout 2026. However, driven by higher-than-anticipated demand for Deep Ultraviolet (DUV) lithography systems and continued strength in the installed-base business, ASML has revised its outlook and now expects non-EUV revenue to experience steady growth.

**Wafer Capacity: Guidance vs. Industry Data**
Historically, ASML guided for consistent, incremental capacity additions of 160,000 wafer starts per month (WSPM) for memory and 200,000 WSPM for logic each year. However, recent independent industry research indicates that actual global wafer starts are already exceeding these baseline projections. This outperformance is particularly pronounced within the memory sector, suggesting that broader industry capacity is expanding at a faster and more aggressive rate than initially modeled in the company's prior guidance.
