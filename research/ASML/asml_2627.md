## Q: What are the business segments of ASML?

Based on the downloaded documents, ASML reports its business in **two main revenue segments** and breaks down by **two end-use markets**:

### Revenue Segments

| Segment | Q1 2026 | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Net System Sales** | €6.3 billion | Sale of new and used lithography systems (67 new + 12 used units in Q1 2026) |
| **Installed Base Management (IBM)** | €2.5 billion | Service, maintenance, and field option/upgrade sales on the ~5,000+ lithography systems already deployed at customer fabs |

### End-Use Market Breakdown

ASML also splits revenue by customer end-use:

| End Market | Description |
|---|---|
| **Logic** | Chipmakers producing processors, SoCs, GPUs (TSMC, Samsung, Intel) — the largest share of system sales |
| **Memory** | DRAM and NAND manufacturers (SK Hynix, Samsung, Micron) |

### Technology Platform Breakdown

Within system sales, the key product lines are:

- **EUV (Extreme Ultraviolet):** The flagship, highest-ASP systems (~€200M+ per unit). Includes the NXE:3800E (230 WpH throughput) and the next-gen High-NA EXE platform
- **DUV (Deep Ultraviolet):** Mature but still essential lithography systems (ArFi, KrF), lower ASP but higher volumes — used across all node types and in trailing-edge fabs

So in summary: **Net System Sales** (EUV + DUV, split by Logic vs. Memory) and **Installed Base Management** (services/upgrades) are the two core business segments. IBM is the higher-margin, recurring revenue stream that ASML is deliberately growing — it reached €2.5B in Q1 alone, up 24% YoY.

## Q: How should I think about the breakup EUV + non-EUV?

### ASML Revenue = 3 Buckets

```
Total Net Sales
├── 1. EUV System Sales (the growth engine)
│     ├── Low-NA (NXE:3600/3800E) — bulk of revenue, ~€200M+ ASP per tool
│     └── High-NA (EXE:5000/5200B) — early stage, ~€350M+ ASP, few units
│
├── 2. Non-EUV System Sales (flattish, ~€25-26B run-rate)
│     ├── ArFi (Immersion) — still strong at leading nodes
│     ├── ArF Dry
│     ├── KrF (incl. NXT:870B at 400+ WpH)
│     ├── I-line
│     └── Metrology & Inspection (growing ~28-30% YoY)
│
└── 3. Installed Base Management (IBM) — recurring, high-margin
      ├── Service contracts (growing as EUV installed base expands)
      └── Upgrades (e.g., 3800E productivity upgrades to 220 WpH)
```

### The numbers that matter (from Q4 2025 call):

| Bucket | FY2025 | FY2026E Guidance | Trend |
|---|---|---|---|
| **EUV revenue** | ~€12B (grew 39% YoY) | "Up significantly" again | 🔼 Steam engine |
| **Non-EUV revenue** | ~€12.5B | "Flattish" at €25-26B combined with IBM | ➡️ Stable |
| **IBM** | €8.2B (grew 26% YoY) | "Up this year" | 🔼 Growing |
| **Total** | €32.7B | €36-40B (midpoint +12%) | 🔼 |

### Key insight from CFO Roger Dassen:

> "The real steam engine behind this growth is once again EUV... Non-EUV business is expected flattish. Within non-EUV, for leading-node customers, DUV goes up a bit, China goes down. Metrology & inspection quite strong."

So the simplest mental model is:
- **EUV drives all incremental growth** — both unit volume and ASP (3800E at 220 WpH costs more than 3600 at 160 WpH)
- **Non-EUV is a stable base** with China declining offset by leading-edge DUV and M&I growth
- **IBM is the hidden compounder** — as the EUV installed base grows, service revenue compounds, and upgrade demand surges when customers want capacity fast (cheaper/faster than waiting for new fabs)

The €36-40B guidance for FY2026 essentially breaks down as: EUV up big → ~€15-17B, Non-EUV flat → ~€12-13B, IBM up → ~€9-10B.

## Q: How many EUV units do they expect to sell in 2026 and 2027, as that decides growth here?

### FY2025 EUV Units: ~44 (confirmed)

Roger Dassen in the Q4 2025 call literally said:

> *"You cannot move from **44 units in 2025 to 80 units in 2026**; doesn't work that way. So you gradually need to crank up your move rate."*

This aligns with the Q2 2025 analyst discussion where François (analyst) said *"below 50... closer to 40 rather than 50"* and Roger confirmed fewer units due to higher productivity 3800E tools (220 WpH = same capacity, fewer boxes, higher ASP).

So my Q4 estimate of ~18-20 EUV units checks out: 11 (Q2) + 9 (Q3) + ~4-6 (Q1, implied) + ~18-20 (Q4) ≈ **42-46 units**.

### FY2026 EUV Units: ~55-65 (estimated)

Management hasn't given an exact number, but we can triangulate:

| Data Point | Source | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| "Can't go from 44 → 80" | Roger, Q4 call | Upper bound is well below 80 |
| "Gradually crank up move rate every quarter" | Roger, Q4 call | Sequential ramp, not a step function |
| EUV revenue guided "up significantly" again | Roger, Q4 call | Probably +30-40% again |
| FY26 total revenue €36-40B | Guidance | EUV needs to be ~€15-17B at midpoint |
| Non-EUV "flattish" at €25-26B | Roger | All incremental growth = EUV |
| NXE:3800E PEP1-E at 230 WpH | Q1 2026 slides | Even higher productivity → fewer units per capacity |
| "2026 is a big year for EUV... more shipments" | CEO Christophe | Volume ramp confirmed |

**Math:**
- FY25 EUV rev: ~€12B on 44 units → blended ASP ~€273M
- FY26 EUV rev: ~€15-17B (at midpoint of total guidance, assuming non-EUV flat + IBM up)
- Blended ASP: ~€250-280M (more Low-NA 3800E at ~€210M, fewer but pricier High-NA at ~€350M+)
- **Implied units: €15-17B ÷ €260M ≈ 58-65 units**

This is a **~40-50% unit increase** from 44, which fits Roger's "gradual ramp" framing.

### FY2027 EUV Units: ~70-80+ (extrapolated)

No explicit guidance yet, but the trajectory points to:
- Continued Low-NA ramp as customers build fabs in 2026 that take tools in 2027
- High-NA **insertion** into HVM starts (~2027-2028 per Christophe)
- More High-NA units mean higher ASP but also higher total revenue
- The "80 unit" number Roger used may have been the 2027 aspiration
- 2030 target of €44-60B implies EUV needs to reach €25-35B, requiring 80-100+ units/year by then

### The Growth Algorithm

```
EUV Revenue Growth = Unit Growth + ASP Growth

FY25: 44 units × €273M ASP = €12.0B
FY26: ~60 units × €265M ASP = ~€15.9B (+33%)
FY27: ~75 units × €275M ASP = ~€20.6B (+30%)
```

The key insight: **ASML's EUV growth is a volume story now, not just an ASP story.** The 3800E productivity upgrades already pushed ASPs up materially in 2024-2025. Going forward, the growth algorithm shifts to shipping more boxes as customers build out mega-fabs. The High-NA ASP premium (~€350M+) provides an additional kicker but with very few units (probably <10/year through 2027).

### The Compounding Machine: IBM

IBM is the most underappreciated segment. Here's why it matters:

```
FY2023: €5.6B
FY2024: €6.5B  (+16%)
FY2025: €8.2B  (+26%)
FY2026: ~€10B  (+22%)  ← Q1 run-rate already confirms
FY2027: ~€12B  (+20%)
```

Every EUV tool ASML ships today generates ~€15-20M/year in recurring service revenue once it exits warranty (typically 1-2 years post-install). With ~44 EUV tools shipped in FY25 and ~60 in FY26, the installed base compounds relentlessly. This is the "hidden annuity" embedded in ASML's business — high-margin, recurring, and growing faster than system sales.

### Summary: Where Does Revenue Growth Come From?

| | FY26E (€38B mid) | FY27E (~€46B mid) |
|---|---|---|
| **EUV growth** | +€2-4B | +€4-6B |
| **IBM growth** | +€2B | +€2-3B |
| **Non-EUV systems** | ~flat | +€0.5-1.5B |
| **Total growth** | +€5-7B | +€7-10B |

**~80% of incremental revenue comes from EUV + IBM.** Non-EUV systems are a stable but stagnant base, held back by China normalization and partially offset by M&I strength. The investment thesis for ASML over the next 2-3 years is entirely about the EUV unit ramp and the IBM compounding effect.
