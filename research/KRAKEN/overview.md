# KRAKEN — Subsea battery

Current scale of the subsea battery business

  - Revenue is not broken out separately — it sits inside the "Products" segment, which was ~$62M in 2025 (total revenue $102.2M less ~$40M of
  services). Within Products, batteries are the largest and fastest-growing of three lines (the others being SAS sensors and KATFISH/sonar). My derived
  estimate puts battery sales in the ~$25–40M range in 2025, but that's a triangulation, not a disclosed figure.
  - Growth has been strong and steady: SeaPower revenue grew ~30% in 2025, and in Q2 2025 it posted its strongest quarter ever in absolute dollars (+26%
  YoY). It was explicitly cited as a primary driver of the record 2025 result.
  - Production is today almost entirely in Germany (Kraken Power GmbH / Kraken Robotik GmbH), which ran "full out" all year. The new Halifax facility
  only began initial production at the very end of 2025 and is ramping through 2026 into 2027 — so it's upside, not yet in the run-rate.
  - Capacity dwarfs current sales: Halifax alone is sized at ~$150M revenue, and combined Germany + Canada at ~$200–250M when fully ramped — implying
  the segment could grow several-fold against the disclosed capacity ceiling before needing more plants.
  - Customer base is concentrated but diversifying: demand has been anchored by one very large XL-UUV customer, but Kraken has added multiple new UUV
  OEM customers since mid-2025, spanning Navy and commercial users globally. Management frames this as winning share and reducing single-customer
  concentration.
  - Economics are attractive: these are high-value, pressure-tolerant deep-sea batteries priced on a dollar-per-kWh basis; management has held margins
  despite customer price pressure by shipping higher-capacity designs.

  Growth levers

  - Capacity unlock (the biggest near-term lever): the Halifax facility more than triples battery production capacity. The business has effectively been
  supply-constrained ("running full out" in Germany), so simply bringing Halifax online converts existing demand into shippable revenue through
  2026–2027.
  - XL UUV program ramp: the structural driver is the emergence of extra-large unmanned underwater vehicles. There are ~8–10 known XL-UUV developers;
  Kraken supplies a major one and is in due-diligence/qualification with most of the others. New customers typically start with small "engineering test
  prototype" battery orders, then scale to production quantities — a built-in ramp.
  - New higher-density product: a new battery design with ~30% higher energy density for large and XL vehicles, which both wins new designs and supports
  re-buys/upgrades from existing customers.
  - New form factors → adjacent market: more compact designs to address the small and medium-sized UUV market — a segment Kraken historically had no
  solution for, expanding the addressable customer pool.
  - New application beyond UUVs — subsea energy storage: an initial partner is using SeaPower batteries in seabed energy-storage systems sold to
  offshore energy markets, opening a second end-market entirely separate from vehicle propulsion.
  - Recurring / replacement dynamic: batteries are the most consumable-like product in the portfolio — finite life plus a growing installed base means
  re-orders and upgrade cycles compound over time rather than tapering after a one-off sale.
  - Outsourcing / share-gain angle: some vehicle makers still build batteries in-house; Kraken's pitch is superior endurance and dedicated focus ("we
  don't build vehicles, we power yours"), converting in-house programs into merchant demand.
  - Defense-spend tailwind: rising NATO/allied naval budgets, protection of undersea infrastructure, and pull-forward of customer requirements
  (customers now citing battery needs "significantly higher than a year ago") underpin demand.
  - Optionality on further capacity: management has already taken extra space in Germany and is evaluating U.S. manufacturing if demand warrants — a
  path to lift the ~$200–250M ceiling.
