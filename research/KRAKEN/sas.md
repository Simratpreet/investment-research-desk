# SAS

Current scale of the business

  - SAS was the fastest-growing product in 2025, up ~60% YoY — outpacing batteries (~30%) and roughly matching services (>60%). It's not disclosed as a
  separate dollar line; it sits inside the ~$62M Products segment alongside batteries and KATFISH.
  - Record unit volumes: the number of SAS units sold in 2025 was almost equal to the total delivered in the prior two years combined, and Q3 2025 set a
  record level of shipments. So volume, not just price, is inflecting.
  - Founding/core technology: Kraken was founded in 2012 around SAS — a lower-cost, ultra-high-resolution seabed imaging/mapping sensor. It's the
  technical heart of the company and the basis for its reputation in mine countermeasures.
  - Scarcity positioning: Kraken describes itself as "one of just a couple of merchant suppliers" of SAS to AUV companies globally. Barriers to entry
  are high (corrosion, extreme pressure, low-bandwidth comms, no underwater GPS), and it competes against only a handful of players.
  - Blue-chip customer base: end users include NATO / Five Eyes navies (US, Royal Danish, Royal Australian, Royal Canadian, Polish) and AUV/UUV OEMs
  like HII, Anduril, and Teledyne — i.e., it sells both directly to navies and as a merchant payload into other people's vehicles.
  - Use case: configurable, high-resolution acoustic imaging for seabed mapping, mine detection, and reconnaissance; compact enough to integrate into a
  wide range of unmanned underwater vehicles.

  Growth levers

  - Riding the UUV/AUV proliferation (the structural lever): as a merchant payload supplier, SAS revenue scales with the broader explosion in autonomous
  underwater vehicle production. Management explicitly ties SAS growth to "the growth of underwater vehicles, AUVs, UUVs" and notes OEM customers are
  seeing strengthening vehicle demand.
  - Retrofit / installed-base upgrades: a large pool of navies and OEMs already have vehicles in the field and want to add a SAS payload — management
  flagged "a lot of extra interest" here. This is incremental demand on top of new-vehicle attach.
  - Mine-countermeasures (MCM) urgency: geopolitical flashpoints (Strait of Hormuz/Middle East, Baltic/Black Sea, Arctic) are driving inbound inquiries;
  protection of critical undersea infrastructure is accelerating MCM investment. Kraken expects to convert "its fair share."
  - New products expanding the addressable use cases:
    - Dual-frequency circular SAS (C-SAS) — 360° single-payload detection/classification/identification, expected to be a US Navy requirement plus a
  need for several other large navies.
    - Longer-range SAS for larger UUVs, with launch-customer discussions underway.
  - Demo-to-program conversion engine: an active in-water demo cadence (US, Netherlands, Italy, New Zealand, UK, Turkey; five navies used Kraken sonar
  at REPMUS in Portugal) seeds future programs of record.
  - Program-of-record pipeline: over a dozen Towfish/SAS programs are in or approaching the RFP stage from navies worldwide, with selections expected
  over the next 2–3 years — a multi-year wave of larger programs than the industry has seen in 5–6 years.
  - Defense-spending tailwind + scarcity pricing power: rising allied naval budgets plus few credible competitors support both volume and margin.
  - Inventory-ready posture: Kraken deliberately used recent capital raises to build SAS inventory and have assets on the shelf, so it can fill urgent
  operational requirements quickly rather than waiting on build-to-order lead times — accelerating revenue conversion.
  - Pull-through with KATFISH: every KATFISH platform carries Kraken SAS, so KATFISH program wins also drag SAS content (and vice versa).

  Caveats

  - Program-driven and lumpy: unlike batteries (recurring/consumable), SAS revenue depends on winning the next program; the broader "sonar" line
  actually fell in 2025 on RMDS/KATFISH timing even as SAS units surged.
  - No standalone disclosure — the ~60% growth and unit commentary are the only quantification; the dollar figure is inside Products.
  - Market size is "tough to frame" per management — there's no clean TAM, just the pipeline and program-of-record count.
