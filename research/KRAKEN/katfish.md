# Katfish

Quick framing: KATFISH is the towed underwater vehicle platform that carries Kraken's SAS and delivers high-speed, high-resolution seabed surveys —
  primarily for mine countermeasures (MCM) and, secondarily, offshore-energy/commercial surveys. LARS (the autonomous launch & recovery systems that
  deploy/retrieve KATFISH from ships and unmanned surface vessels) is the natural attachment, so I've grouped them.

  Current scale of the business

  - This was the weak pillar in 2025. Management openly called KATFISH/sonar "the disappointing one versus our original expectations" — orders slipped
  at year-end and the broader "sonar" revenue line declined, dragged by KATFISH timing plus the wind-down of the Canadian Navy RMDS (Remote Mine
  Disposal System) integration project. In Q2 2025 the combined sonar line fell >50% YoY.
  - Lumpy, program-driven, capital-equipment revenue. KATFISH activity "can vary year to year due to the natural procurement cycle" — these are
  big-ticket platform sales tied to discrete navy programs, not recurring orders. Not separately disclosed in dollars; sits inside the ~$62M Products
  segment.
  - Inflecting now: management says KATFISH is "turning the corner" and expects a strong 2026, citing a recent order from the Polish Navy and a surge of
  inbound MCM interest.
  - Reputation/positioning: Kraken has demonstrated KATFISH + SAS with leading NATO / Five Eyes navies (Danish, Australian, US, Canadian, Dutch,
  Italian, NZ) and has a solid MCM reputation — it expects to win its "fair share" of a large coming wave.

  Growth levers

  - A multi-year wave of programs of record (the big lever): there are over a dozen Towfish programs of record currently in or approaching the RFP stage
  from navies worldwide, with selections expected over the next 2–3 years — a cycle management characterizes as "significantly larger than what the
  industry has seen over the last 5–6 years."
  - Mine-countermeasures urgency: MCM is the primary KATFISH application, and current flashpoints (Strait of Hormuz / Middle East, plus Baltic,
  Indo-Pacific, Arctic) are creating urgent operational requirements and heavy customer inquiry — pulling demand forward.
  - Shift to unmanned surface vessels (USVs) via new LARS: Kraken developed a significantly smaller, lighter LARS that "opens the aperture" to integrate
  KATFISH onto a far wider range of vessels of opportunity and USVs (rather than only large crewed ships). It's demoing this with a large defense prime
  and a European navy — this expands the deployable platform base and unlocks USV-centric programs. (The LARS team is the ex–Rolls-Royce Marine
  engineering group in Halifax.)
  - SAS pull-through: every KATFISH carries Kraken SAS, so KATFISH wins also drive sensor content — the two products reinforce each other.
  - Inventory-ready posture: Kraken deliberately built KATFISH inventory/assets on the shelf so it can respond fast to urgent navy requirements instead
  of building to order — accelerating conversion of today's heightened demand.
  - Commercial/offshore-energy crossover (an underexploited lever): historically every commercial KATFISH ended up sold into the higher-priority defense
  market. With capacity ramping, Kraken delivered a service-dedicated KATFISH to the UK (in the water for customer demos from late 2025) to finally
  build a commercial survey revenue stream — a second end-market for the same platform.
  - Demo-to-award engine: an active demo cadence (REPMUS Portugal, plus in-water demos across the US, Netherlands, Italy, NZ, UK, Turkey) seeds the
  program-of-record pipeline.
  - Defense-spend tailwind: rising allied naval budgets and infrastructure-protection mandates underpin the whole MCM category.

  Caveats

  - Lumpiest of the segments — revenue swings on a handful of program awards and percentage-of-completion timing; a single slipped order materially
  moves the year (as 2025 showed).
  - Conversion risk: the "dozen+ programs of record" are pipeline, not bookings — they convert over 2–3 years and Kraken competes with a few others for
  each.
  - No standalone disclosure and no clean market size ("tough to frame," per management).
  - Recovery is guidance/expectation-based — the 2026 strength depends on demos and negotiations crossing the line.

  Net: KATFISH is the highest-torque, highest-variance lever in the portfolio — it underdelivered in 2025 but sits in front of the largest MCM program
  cycle in years, with the new USV-focused LARS and a nascent commercial channel as additional upside.
