- They are installing the extreme plumbing—the "cryo and warm lines"—to circulate freezing and room-temperature fluids exactly where they need to go. Second, they are repairing the giant thermos's heavy-duty insulation—the "lower cryostat thermal shield"—to ensure the machine's outer magnets stay frozen even when the inside gets hotter than the sun. Finally, they are custom-making precision metal wedges—the "bio shield shimming plates"—to perfectly level and securely balance the massive concrete walls that protect humans from the reactor's radiation
- INOX India successfully chilled a testing station for the reactor's giant, super-powerful magnets down to a mind-boggling 4 Kelvin—about -269 degrees Celsius, which is colder than deep space and just barely above absolute zero. On top of proving they can handle these extreme freezing temperatures, they also successfully maneuvered "Sector 3"—a colossal, multi-ton, pie-shaped slice of the donut-shaped fusion machine—and safely lowered it into the "Tokamak pit," the central cavern where the reactor is being assembled. In short, they successfully tested the extreme refrigeration needed to keep the machine from melting, and then carefully dropped a massive puzzle piece right into the heart of the world's most complex science experiment.
- The **Tokamak Pit** is the specialized, seismically isolated machine well within the ITER Tokamak Complex in France, where the giant fusion reactor is currently being assembled.

### Regulatory changes for logistic companies acting as tailwind for INOX India
**The "Before" (What used to be the rule):**
Previously, safety regulations dictated that if a truck was hauling a giant pressure vessel full of hazardous or cryogenic industrial gases (Tank 1), it was not allowed to be powered by an LNG fuel tank (Tank 2). Regulators didn't want a pressurized fuel system operating right next to a massive pressurized cargo payload. Therefore, all these specialized tanker trucks had to run on standard diesel fuel.

**The "After" (What the rule changed to):**
The regulatory bodies (PNGRB, PESO, and RTO) updated their safety guidelines. They now allow trucks that carry pressure vessels to actually run on LNG.

**Why this is a big deal for INOX:**
Because of this rule change, INOX India can now sell their LNG fuel tanks to logistics companies that transport industrial gases. They immediately took advantage of this by taking a standard diesel tanker truck from their sister company (INOX Air Products), ripping out the diesel tank, and successfully retrofitting it with their own LNG fuel tank. If this test truck performs well, it opens up a massive new market to retrofit entire fleets of industrial gas trucks.
